How It Works

Most engagements run three to six months, in three phases.

The shape stays the same whether the work is finding a first product or fixing a growth problem. What changes is where I spend the time.

01

Strategy

I get into the actual problem first. Customer research, market research, a clear look at what's working and what isn't. No recommendations before I understand what's really going on.

02

Build

I either execute the plan myself or bring in a small team to do it, depending on what the work needs. Either way, someone senior stays close to the work the whole time.

03

Handoff

Before I leave, I help define what the permanent role should look like, if there needs to be one at all. You get through the gap without carrying a full time hire before you know if you need one, and without getting stuck if the engagement doesn't work out.

If you've been burned by fractional hires before

A lot of fractional engagements fail because the person shows up with a generic playbook and no real involvement in the work. I don't hand you a deck and disappear. I do the research myself, I build the product myself when that's what's needed, and I stay accountable for outcomes, not just recommendations.

How I Can Help

Four situations founders bring me into.

Where I come in depends on where the company is. Here's what that looks like at each stage.

Pre-seed

Turning a messy idea into a real product and business case

You have an idea you believe in, but you can't yet say clearly who it's for, what to build first, or whether it's actually a business. I run customer and market research, figure out the real product to build first, and tell you honestly if the idea needs to change before you spend money building it.

I've done this from a one line brief before, once I was handed nothing more than a vague mandate to build something in personalisation and trust, and turned it into a full product, a proprietary matching algorithm that took the resulting app past 500,000 downloads. On another project, a founder needed a real revenue strategy with almost no direction to start from, and I built the pricing and monetisation model from scratch. On a third, the research showed a founder's target age group was wrong and the core idea needed to change. We caught that before any engineering work started.

Before that, I ran my own consulting practice, Graph++, for three years. I worked on more than 20 projects across six countries, mostly taking founders from an unclear idea to a plan they could actually build.

Series A and B

Scaling what's working, fixing what isn't

You've found something that works, but growth has slowed and you're not sure which change will actually move the numbers. Maybe it's a new revenue stream. Maybe it's a broken signup flow. Maybe it's pricing. I do the research to find where things are actually breaking, then build the fix.

At ZebPay, I did this for five years. I redesigned onboarding and cut the time it took users to complete verification from 10 minutes to under 1. That helped grow the user base from 3.5 million to 7 million. I launched three new revenue products from scratch. One of them hit 18 million dollars in daily trading volume within its first 30 days and became the platform's top revenue source. Another grew daily trading volume by 112 percent. A third added 20 crore rupees in assets within days of launch. I also designed a feature that would give users personalised, risk aware guidance on their portfolio based on market conditions.

As a fractional advisor, this is the same work at a smaller scale. I come in, find out where growth is actually stuck, and either fix it myself or set up a small team to do it. You get this without paying for a full time hire before you know if you need one.

Operations

Automating internal work, before and with AI

Your team is doing manual work that's eating hours it shouldn't. Scheduling, intake, research, approvals. I've spent years fixing this kind of problem, first by redesigning how the work gets done, and now by building AI systems that do parts of the work directly.

Earlier in my career, I automated scheduling, prescriptions, and billing for a clinic seeing more than 10,000 patients a month. I also designed the process for a digital lending platform that disbursed 180 crore rupees across 70,000 loans in its first six months.

More recently, I built an AI system with six different agents that each handle one part of turning founder interviews into product insight. One agent handles intake, another does the synthesis, another checks for weak or overstated claims. I built it as my own tool, then used it on a real project. It saved more than 15 hours of manual work and the findings changed the founder's product direction.

AI product strategy

Designing AI into the product itself

You know your product needs AI somewhere, but "add AI" isn't a plan. You need to know what it should actually do for the user, where it fits, and whether it changes how you make money. I work out that strategy, and where it makes sense, build a real prototype people can try, not just a slide deck.

I've conceptualised and built AI product prototypes across health, travel, education, research funding, and portfolio personalisation. These are a few examples. One is a fitness app that turns a person's goals and equipment into a personalised training plan, built for a founder who couldn't find guidance suited to her stage of recovery. Another is a travel planning tool that weighs a traveller's preferences and limits to recommend real options. A third is a research funding platform connecting donors, foundations, and research institutions, where I defined the pricing model and built the working version myself, so the founder could test real demand before committing to engineering costs. I also designed a feature that would give users personalised, risk aware guidance on their portfolio based on market conditions.
